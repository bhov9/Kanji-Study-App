# Sources, permissions and snapshot boundaries

All files in this directory are research outputs for bhov9/Kanji-Study-App. The Jiten downloads were retrieved on 25 November 2026 UTC. The local dictionary and government inputs retain the source versions and checksums in the [original manifest](../sources.json) and [supplement manifest](../supplement/manifest.json). The existing production audit uses Git object d70055576c40b91fd40f7180f4e7d27f3d9f27a3. The research checkout has an older production ancestor.

**Jiten.** The publisher explicitly licenses its downloadable vocabulary and kanji frequency lists under CC BY-SA 4.0. Credit Jiten (jiten.moe) and the dictionary projects it acknowledges. The published corpus description at acquisition was approximately 4.09 billion characters from 19,294 media titles. Release assets use rolling URLs without a semantic release number. The manifest identifies each snapshot by retrieval time, server filename, byte length and SHA-256 of the original uncompressed CSV. The repository adds lossless gzip compression. Media IDs in local filenames map to the server's category name in the manifest. The supplement supplies no raw media text. [Frequency lists and license](https://jiten.moe/frequency-dictionaries) · [Provenance](https://jiten.moe/about) · [API guide](https://jiten.moe/guides/using-the-api) · [Exact files](jiten/manifest.json)

The vocabulary columns are Word, Form and Rank. Different written forms and pronunciations can appear on separate rows. Jiten lists 十 with じゅう and also with other form strings. It supplies no JMdict entry ID, sense ID, document count, occurrence count or per-million rate in these CSVs. The kanji CSV supplies character and rank. Interpret ranks as the publisher's ordering within each list; the files cannot establish the exact occurrence-versus-document weighting. The category lists measure relative position in different populations and do not sum to a global count. Potential overlaps include Japanese YouTube subtitles with TUBELEX, subtitle exposure with wordfreq, and literature domains with Shiberu. Matching titles and document-level overlap are unknown.

**Dictionary and government data.** JMdict, KANJIDIC2 and KRADFILE are copyright EDRDG and their contributors. JMdict Simplified by scriptin supplies the JSON conversion. JmdictFurigana by Doublevil supplies word segmentation under the dictionary data license. The research exports select and join these data, including written forms, definitions, readings and eligible KANJIDIC2 fields. Restricted SKIP codes and the full example-sentence corpus are excluded from publication. Preserve attribution, ShareAlike obligations, and update requirements for deployed dictionary use. Government readings, notes, examples and school stages were adapted from the Agency for Cultural Affairs and MEXT; links, permission terms and transformations are in the supplement. [EDRDG license](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html) · [Furigana](https://github.com/Doublevil/JmdictFurigana#licence) · [Government sources](../supplement/README.md)

**Other public frequency data.** Shiberu Japanese Word Frequency List 2026 by Shiberu, CC BY 4.0; retain its upstream Wikipedia, Aozora Bunko and Tatoeba credits. TUBELEX by Adam Nohejl and colleagues, BSD-3-Clause notice included. Japanese wordfreq 3.1.1 by Robyn Speer and contributors, CC BY-SA 4.0 data. This research adds rank combinations, comparisons and candidate selection. Redistribute combined public data under CC BY-SA 4.0 and retain the individual source notices. **Local validation.** CEJC, BCCWJ and NINJAL educational word lists remain local. The public validation report contains aggregate intersections only. The NINJAL v1.1 short- and long-unit archives and the educational CD list CC BY-NC-ND 3.0. No restricted per-word ranks, textbook counts or derived curricular weights are published. [NINJAL file-specific terms](../supplement/README.md)

**VDRJ.** Newly acquired locally for validation. Tatsuhiko Matsushita (2011), Vocabulary Database for Reading Japanese, version 1.0, teachers edition. The publisher's landing page also describes later research editions; the actual teacher download and workbook citation specify 1.0. The main worksheet contains 60,894 rows plus a header. The 14,631,430-byte workbook has SHA-256 22dde654b967dde38ce93c0532a7b5d730a4d7b2c16b7b840a4aa89008500173. The author requests citation for research use. Public redistribution permission for this workbook was not established. The file is `../local/VDRJ_Ver1_0_Teachers_Top60894.xlsx`. [Publisher](https://www.tatsumatsushita.com/database.html) · [Teacher workbook](https://www.tatsumatsushita.com/database/VDRJ_Ver1_0_Teachers_Top60894.xlsx)

**JEV.** The authors describe 17,908 headwords in version 1.1. Their site limits use to education and research, requires acknowledgment and asks users to refrain from redistribution. The Excel download requires an information form and then an ID/password. The publisher also provides a view-only sheet. No JEV entries were collected or used to score words. [Project and access terms](https://jhlee.sakura.ne.jp/JEV/)
